Frequently asked questions

How the tester works, what it measures and — just as important — what it cannot measure. If your question is not here, the contact page is the fastest way to reach us.

Does the tester record what I type?

No. Every key event is handled by JavaScript inside your browser and only anonymous counters are updated. The characters you type are never transmitted, never stored and never logged. Reloading the page clears the test.

Is the tool free?

Yes. The keyboard and mouse tester has no paid tier, no account and no installation. The site may show advertising to cover hosting costs, but the tool itself always stays free and complete.

Which keys can the browser never detect?

Keys that the operating system reserves for itself: system shortcuts, the Windows key on many configurations, function keys handled by the firmware, and manufacturer hotkeys. They are intercepted before any application, including the browser, can see them.

Why does a key stay highlighted after I release it?

Usually the browser never got the key-up event, which happens when the window loses focus while a key is held. Click on the page, press the Reset test button, or switch windows and back to clear the state.

Does the tester work with all keyboard layouts?

Yes. Keys are identified by their physical position using the code property of the keyboard event, so QWERTY, AZERTY, QWERTZ and other layouts are all supported. The layout drawn on screen is the standard US ANSI reference.

Can I test an external keyboard on my phone or tablet?

Yes. Connect a Bluetooth or USB keyboard to the device, open this page in the browser and type. The virtual keyboard shows the physical keys being pressed. The on-screen keyboard of the device itself does not generate real key events, so a phone without an external keyboard cannot test itself.

Why are Ctrl, Cmd or Alt combinations not counted?

Because those combinations are browser and system shortcuts. Counting them would break refresh, copy, paste and window management, so the tester ignores the combination and only counts the modifier key itself. The modifiers that are currently held are shown under the keyboard.

What does the coverage percentage mean?

Coverage is the share of the 104-key ANSI layout that you have pressed at least once. Repeatedly pressing the same key increases the total press counter but never the unique key counter, so the percentage cannot be inflated by hitting one key many times.

How is the double-click detection calculated?

The tester measures the time between two consecutive presses of the same button and checks that the pointer barely moved. Under 500 milliseconds the pair is reported as a double-click, matching the usual operating system threshold. Intervals close to the limit are flagged as slow.

My mouse does not have a middle button. What should the counter show?

Zero, and that is the expected result. Many mice have no middle button, and some laptop trackpads have no middle click at all. The tester counts what your hardware actually reports.

Why is the right-click menu blocked inside the test area?

So the right button can be counted. The context menu is suppressed only inside the test box; everywhere else on the page the right click behaves exactly as usual.

Does the mouse test detect the scroll wheel direction correctly?

Yes, the wheel readout shows whether the last movement was up, down, left or right, and the scroll counter accumulates the distance reported by the operating system. Note that high-resolution wheels report many small steps instead of one step per notch.

Can a browser test prove that my keyboard is healthy?

No. It proves that the operating system delivers the key events to the page. Worn switches, an intermittent cable, a broken keyboard matrix or a failing controller can still produce correct events while the hardware feels wrong.

Does the site use cookies?

The tester itself does not set tracking cookies. When advertising is enabled, this site may use cookies and similar technologies of Google AdSense, and your consent choice is stored locally in your browser. You can change that choice at any time through the cookie settings link in the footer.

Does the page work without an internet connection?

The tester itself does, once the page is loaded: all detection happens locally and no request is made while you test. Advertising obviously requires a connection.

Why does the keyboard not match the layout I use?

The on-screen keyboard is a fixed reference of the standard 104-key ANSI layout. Because detection is based on physical positions, your AZERTY or QWERTZ keyboard still lights up the correct physical keys, but the printed characters belong to the US layout.

Are the side buttons of my mouse supported?

Yes, buttons 4 and 5 are counted when your mouse has them and the operating system forwards them. Some mice expose more than five buttons; the tester counts the two most common extra buttons.

How do I reset the test?

Use the Reset test button above the keyboard or inside the mouse area. Every counter returns to zero and the highlighted keys are cleared. Reloading the page has the same effect.
